# Expanded Luna low / Sol low comparison — September 23, 2026

**Completed 20 additional paired live-analysis cases: 40 real API responses, all complete and structurally valid.** Sol offers more guidance and sometimes handles interpersonal context better, but also introduces unsupported interpretations. This is not a clean accuracy win. Product prompts and defaults remain unchanged.

Sol's observed median was about **11% shorter**; it was faster in 15 of the 20 pairs. Sol cost **17.8x** as much. Both arms had exactly 67,423 input tokens, 34,440 cached reads and 32,923 cache writes, so unequal aggregate caching does not explain this comparison's cost gap. Sol's lower output/reasoning token usage partly offsets its higher token prices. Reply/cue counts measure availability, not correctness. Weak calm hypotheses persist in both, more often in Sol.

Current official standard per-million input/cached/cache-write/output prices: [Luna](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna) $0.10/$0.01/$0.125/$0.50; [Sol](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-sol) $2/$0.20/$2.50/$10. Costs use raw returned usage and these rates, not a reconciled invoice. Reasoning tokens are included in output billing, never counted twice.

## Case-by-case qualitative review

All cases are from the other saved call, identified as `recent` in the historical archive. They are distinct checkpoints, not 20 independent conversations. Judgments are non-blinded assistant assessments against the frozen request, not human gold labels or an accuracy percentage. Later transcript turns were not used to excuse an unsupported prediction at an earlier checkpoint.

| Checkpoint / situation | Comparison |
|---|---|
| 00 — sparse speech and frozen video | Both appropriately abstain; no useful difference. |
| 01 — which project column holds a card | Both infer calm from a practical question; neither invents a location. Weak affect grounding in both. |
| 04 — task difficulty question | Sol waits for the contact to answer the user's question. Luna asserts the user cannot see a level, which the supplied text does not establish. Sol is better on reply grounding; both add weak calm. |
| 08 — transcription trouble and remaining usage | Both avoid repeating an already answered usage question, but label it calm from wording alone. Broadly comparable. |
| 09 — source of release news | Sol recognizes a request for the source and offers to send the tweet; Luna abstains on replies. Sol gives a plausible optional next step, but the source was already explained and its usefulness is subjective. |
| 10 — ambiguous “What?” after model speculation | Both identify uncertainty. Sol invents that the model name was “just a joke.” Luna calls it a guess and says probably not literally that name; closer to tentative wording, but also supplies an unstated intention. Neither is fully grounded. |
| 15 — quoted app guidance and ambiguous “mouth” | Luna asks what the contact noticed. Sol asserts the app detected mouth movement, which this ambiguous fragment does not establish. Luna is safer and more grounded. |
| 16 — mouse/mouth clarification | Sol offers a plausible “Where?” follow-up; Luna abstains. Both weakly infer calm. More engagement versus caution, not a clear accuracy difference. |
| 19 — playful hearing disagreement | Sol recognizes plausible humor and offers a playful reply; Luna abstains. Sol adds a weak calm label and its reply citations omit the clearest earlier “mouse” turn. Useful contextual reading with imperfect evidence selection. |
| 24 — laptop-at-red-light joke | Both recognize possible joking, though Luna labels sarcasm and Sol humor. Both suggested replies play along with distracting laptop use while driving; flag as undesirable/ambiguous guidance rather than a safety success. No real driving habit is established by the banter. |
| 26 — poor visibility | Sol suggests moving into better light, a practical response grounded in the broader supplied visibility discussion; Luna abstains. Sol's cited IDs do not include the clearest darkness explanation. Utility gain, imperfect citation selection, weak calm in both. |
| 29 — “I'm not talking to her” | Sol repeats the user's preceding explanation about a third party; Luna offers no reply. Little added value from Sol here. Both add calm to a boundary statement. |
| 30 — reluctance to deal with a disagreement | Sol tentatively recognizes frustration; Luna abstains on affect. Both offer a no-pressure response. Sol adds the user's burden of mediating; supported by the surrounding complaint, but more emotionally loaded. Mixed calibration/usefulness. |
| 32 — “I'm not mad at her” qualification | Both recognize strain; Sol explicitly preserves the boundary and not-mad qualification and asks whether help is wanted. Luna offers generic validation. Sol gives more precise interpersonal guidance in this sample. |
| 33 — self-respect versus childishness | Sol preserves discomfort with someone else being mad and avoids repeating the user's acknowledgment. Luna describes being upset with her despite the earlier denial and repeats an acknowledgment. Sol handles the distinction better. |
| 35 — reviewing generated code | Both give weak matter-of-fact/calm interpretations and no reply. No meaningful difference. |
| 38 — marketing ownership | Both offer grounded user-voice replies. Sol more closely preserves the stated client-acquisition/cost concern, while Luna asks for ownership. Sol's citations omit the exact commercial-reference turn used in its reply. Mixed specificity/citation tradeoff. |
| 39 — agreement about responsibilities | Sol restates the need for a client plan; Luna suggests explicitly asking for priorities and a plan. Both are plausible suggested actions, not established commitments. Both infer calm from agreement. No clear accuracy winner. |
| 44 — praise and closing | Both correctly retain supportive praise. Sol adds a conventional thanks; Luna abstains. Extra reply availability, not a demonstrated accuracy gain. |
| 46 — contact thanks | Both offer an appropriate acknowledgment with correct speaker direction. Both infer calm from the short closing. Unlike the earlier call's user-thanks error, this is the contact thanking the user. |

**Interpretation:** Sol's added detail is sometimes worthwhile, especially preserving qualifications and suggesting concrete next steps. It is not consistently safer: it also invents an interpretation of ambiguous app text and an unstated joke. Luna avoids some of those additions but can invent user observations or lose interpersonal nuance. More output and valid JSON do not establish better accuracy.

Keep Luna low as the economical live default, with Sol low as the higher-cost option. No switch was implemented. These 20 requests per model test only live analysis; they do not extend the earlier extraction/summary findings or independently establish that Sol is better for every task.

## Method, limits and evidence

- One fresh low-effort answer per model for each frozen case; no retries, fallback, repair or post-result tuning. These are saved development cases, not a held-out benchmark. No previous answer/expected assessment was sent to either model.
- Original September 15 requests retain exact input/instructions/schema/verbosity and 1,200-token live cap. They are not the current 1,800-token/four-item UI contract. Null-speaker rows and numerical audio/visual observations remain unchanged. No fresh transcription, retrieval, camera analysis, paced scheduling or GUI integration.
- Twenty selected checkpoints: 00, 01, 04, 08, 09, 10, 15, 16, 19, 24, 26, 29, 30, 32, 33, 35, 38, 39, 44, 46. Selected for varied content before outputs were known. Serial requests, alternating model order for the first 19 pairs; the isolated final continuation ran Luna then Sol. Complete HTTP-response wall times, not first-token times. One response/case cannot measure repeat stability.
- Private evidence: `build/gpt6-luna-sol-20-20260923/` and `build/gpt6-luna-sol-20-finish-20260923/`. Prepared manifests, frozen rubric/cases, exact requests/raw responses, timestamps, reservations, results and scripts remain Git-ignored. Source input hashes match the historical manifest; prior result hashes remain unchanged.
- Both runners' offline audits passed exact request/model/schema/parsed-output integrity and independently recalculated raw usage. `combine.py` passed exact 20-pair coverage, absence of duplicated pairs, unchanged original source requests, supplied citation-ID membership and cumulative-ledger reconciliation. Valid citation IDs do not prove semantic support; the case review records citation weaknesses separately.

No production changes, release acceptance, automatic further testing, commit or push is implied. The new cap is cumulative, not a fresh allowance per run.
